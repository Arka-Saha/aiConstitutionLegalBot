-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-1] Article 14 — Right to Equality
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or
equal protection of the laws within the territory of India. This
means the law applies equally to everyone, regardless of status —
no one is above the law, and no one can be discriminated against
by the law.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-2] Article 15 — Prohibition of Discrimination
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The State cannot discriminate against any citizen on grounds of
religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. Access to shops,
restaurants, public wells, roads, and places of public entertainment
cannot be restricted based on these grounds. Special provisions can
be made for women, children, and socially/educationally backward
classes (e.g., reservations).

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-3] Article 19 — Right to Freedom
-------------------------------------------------------------------
All citizens have the right to:
(a) Freedom of speech and expression
(b) Assemble peacefully without arms
(c) Form associations or unions
(d) Move freely throughout India
(e) Reside and settle in any part of India
(g) Practice any profession or carry on any occupation, trade, or business
These rights are subject to "reasonable restrictions" the State can
impose for public order, security, decency, or morality.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-4] Article 21 — Right to Life and Personal Liberty
-------------------------------------------------------------------
No person shall be deprived of their life or personal liberty except
according to procedure established by law. Courts have expanded this
to include: right to privacy, right to a clean environment, right to
livelihood, right to shelter, right to health, and right to a speedy
trial. This is one of the most litigated and protective rights in
the Constitution.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-5] Article 22 — Protection Against Arrest and Detention
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Any person who is arrested has the right to:
1. Be informed of the grounds of arrest as soon as possible
2. Consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of their choice
3. Be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours of
   arrest (excluding travel time)
4. Not be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate authorization
These protections do not apply to enemy aliens or persons arrested
under preventive detention laws (which have separate safeguards).

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[FR-6] Article 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Any citizen can directly approach the Supreme Court if their
Fundamental Rights are violated. The Court can issue writs:
Habeas Corpus (produce a detained person), Mandamus (order to
perform public duty), Prohibition (stop lower court overreach),
Certiorari (transfer case/quash order), Quo Warranto (challenge
illegal holding of public office). Dr. Ambedkar called this the
"heart and soul" of the Constitution.

CRIMINAL PROCEDURE — FIR & ARREST RIGHTS (BNSS 2023)
===================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CR-1] Filing an FIR (First Information Report)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Anyone can file an FIR at the police station having jurisdiction
  over where the crime occurred — for cognizable offenses (serious
  crimes like theft, assault, murder), police MUST register it.
- Police cannot refuse to register an FIR for a cognizable offense.
  If they refuse, the complainant can:
  1. Send the complaint in writing to the Superintendent of Police
  2. File a complaint before the Magistrate under BNSS
  3. Approach the State Human Rights Commission or file online (many
     states have e-FIR portals for certain offenses)
- A "Zero FIR" can be filed at ANY police station regardless of
  jurisdiction, which is then transferred to the correct station.
- The complainant is entitled to a free copy of the FIR after registration.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CR-2] Rights During Arrest
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Right to know the grounds of arrest immediately.
- Right to inform a friend/relative/person of choice about the
  arrest and location of detention.
- Right to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours.
- Right to consult a lawyer of choice; if unable to afford one,
  entitled to free legal aid.
- Women can only be arrested by/in the presence of a woman police
  officer, and generally not after sunset or before sunrise except
  in exceptional circumstances with prior permission.
- Right to a medical examination to record injuries at time of arrest.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CR-3] Bail Basics
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bailable offenses: bail is a matter of right, police/court must grant it.
- Non-bailable offenses: bail is at court's discretion, based on
  severity of offense, risk of flight, tampering with evidence, etc.
- Anticipatory bail can be sought BEFORE arrest if a person fears
  arrest in a non-bailable offense (filed in Sessions Court/High Court).

RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT, 2005
===================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[RTI-1] How to File an RTI Application
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Any citizen can file an RTI application to any public authority
  (government department, PSU, government-funded body) requesting
  information.
- Application must be addressed to the Public Information Officer
  (PIO) of the relevant department, in writing (or electronically
  where available), in English/Hindi/local official language.
- Fee: Rs. 10 (varies slightly by state); no fee for BPL applicants.
- Application should clearly state what information is sought — no
  need to give reasons for the request.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[RTI-2] Response Timelines
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Standard requests: PIO must respond within 30 days.
- If information concerns life or liberty of a person: must respond
  within 48 hours.
- If application is transferred to another department: timeline
  extends by 5 days.
- If PIO fails to respond in time, it is deemed a refusal — applicant
  can file a First Appeal within 30 days to the appellate authority
  in the same department.
- If unsatisfied with the First Appeal outcome, a Second Appeal can
  be filed with the State/Central Information Commission.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[RTI-3] What Cannot Be Sought
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Information exempted under Section 8 includes: matters affecting
national security/sovereignty, information forbidden by court,
trade secrets, personal information with no public interest,
information that would endanger someone's life/safety, and Cabinet
papers (until decision is made).

CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019
===================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CP-1] Consumer Rights
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Every consumer has the right to:
1. Safety — protection against goods/services hazardous to life
2. Information — right to know quality, quantity, price of goods
3. Choice — access to a variety of goods at competitive prices
4. Be Heard — right to file complaints and be heard
5. Redressal — right to seek compensation for unfair practices
6. Consumer Education

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CP-2] Filing a Consumer Complaint
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Complaints can be filed at District, State, or National Consumer
  Disputes Redressal Commission depending on the value of goods/
  services and compensation claimed:
  - District Commission: up to Rs. 1 crore
  - State Commission: Rs. 1 crore to Rs. 10 crore
  - National Commission: above Rs. 10 crore
- Can be filed online via the E-Daakhil portal (edaakhil.nic.in).
- No court fee for claims up to Rs. 5 lakh.
- Complaint should include: details of the seller, nature of
  defect/deficiency, proof of purchase, and relief sought.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[CP-3] E-Commerce Specific Protections
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- E-commerce platforms must display total price, delivery timelines,
  return/refund/exchange policies clearly.
- Prohibits unfair trade practices like false advertising, refusal
  to take back defective goods, and flash-sale manipulation.
- Consumers can report grievances to the platform's designated
  Grievance Officer, who must acknowledge within 48 hours and
  resolve within 1 month.

WOMEN'S SAFETY LAWS
===================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[WS-1] Workplace Sexual Harassment (POSH Act, 2013)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every workplace with 10+ employees must have an Internal
  Complaints Committee (ICC).
- Complaint must be filed within 3 months of the incident (extendable
  by 3 more months if valid reason shown).
- ICC must complete inquiry within 90 days and the employer must
  act on recommendations within 60 days of receiving the report.
- Complainant can seek interim relief (transfer, leave) during inquiry.
- If no ICC exists, complaint can be filed with the Local Complaints
  Committee (LCC) at the district level.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[WS-2] Domestic Violence Protection (PWDVA, 2005)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Covers physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, and economic abuse
  by a spouse, partner, or family member.
- A woman can file a complaint with a Protection Officer, Police,
  or directly approach a Magistrate.
- Reliefs available: Protection Order (stops further abuse),
  Residence Order (right to stay in the shared household),
  Monetary Relief (compensation, maintenance), Custody Order.
- Complaints can be filed regardless of whether the woman has
  initiated any other legal proceeding (e.g., divorce).

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[WS-3] Emergency Contacts (Women & General Safety)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Women's Helpline: 181
- Police Emergency: 112
- National Commission for Women: 011-26942369
- Domestic Violence Helpline: 181 / 1091

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[LA-1] Free Legal Aid Eligibility (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Free legal aid is available through NALSA/State Legal Services
Authority (SLSA) to:
- Women and children
- Persons with disabilities
- SC/ST community members
- Victims of trafficking or beggar
- Industrial workmen
- Persons in custody (including in a protective home)
- Persons with annual income below the state-specified limit
  (varies by state, commonly Rs. 1-3 lakh/year)
- Victims of mass disaster, violence, flood, drought, earthquake

-------------------------------------------------------------------
[LA-2] How to Access Free Legal Aid
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Apply at the nearest District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)
  office, Taluka Legal Services Committee, or via NALSA's website
  (nalsa.gov.in).
- Legal aid clinics operate in most district courts and many
  villages via Panel Lawyers.
- NALSA Helpline: 15100 (toll-free)
- Services include: free lawyer representation, court fee waiver,
  document/affidavit drafting help, and mediation/Lok Adalat access.
